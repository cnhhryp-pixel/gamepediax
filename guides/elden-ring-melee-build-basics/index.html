Elden Ring Melee Build Basics

A spoiler-light Elden Ring melee build guide focused on weapon comfort, survivability, stamina discipline and coherent stat investment.

A good melee build starts with a weapon you enjoy and enough survivability to stay close long enough to learn spacing and timing.

Choose a weapon you can use consistently

Moveset comfort matters because melee builds spend more time managing distance and timing directly.

Build enough survivability to make mistakes

Learning close-range fights is easier when one missed dodge does not end the attempt immediately.

Respect stamina and action economy

Attacking, dodging and blocking all compete for the same decision space.

Keep stat investment coherent

Support the main weapon and core combat loop before adding unrelated damage paths.

Use a three-part melee loop

A practical melee loop is approach, commit, recover. Enter at a range where the weapon can threaten, choose a short attack sequence you can safely finish, then preserve enough stamina and space to react. If the build only works when every attack lands perfectly, the problem is usually not damage—it is that the loop has too little recovery margin.

Melee build FAQ

Should a melee build maximize damage as early as possible?

No. Early survivability and consistency can create more real damage because you stay alive long enough to use the weapon.

How do I know if a weapon is too slow for me?

If you repeatedly get punished during recovery and dislike the timing, a faster or lower-commitment style may fit better.

What is the most common early build mistake?

Spreading levels across too many unrelated damage options before the main weapon and survival baseline are established.
